> A longer version of a note I [originally shared on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7488955797054496768/).

We're currently hiring a Product Designer at Transect, which means design capacity
is tight for the moment. When our Director of Product, Gabe Wolpa, shared
wireframes for an upcoming feature, there was a gap between the early concept and
something the engineering team could confidently build. So I jumped in to help
translate the wireframes into a direction that fits our existing UI.

Here's how I approached it. I used ChatGPT to iterate on the visual direction,
feeding it screenshots of our current product so the suggestions stayed grounded
in what we actually have. Then I used Claude's Figma integration to rebuild the
concepts with our existing components and interaction patterns — not a fresh
design language, but the one the team already knows and ships against.

The goal was never to replace design expertise. It was to reduce ambiguity and
help the team keep moving, without producing a UI that engineering would later
have to reinterpret or rebuild from scratch.

## Where AI actually helps

From an engineering leadership perspective, this is exactly the kind of place AI
earns its keep: connecting early product ideas to the realities of an established
application — its design system, its components, its implementation process. The
value isn't "AI made a picture." The value is that the picture is *compatible*
with the system it has to live in.

The result was a more implementation-ready direction, tighter alignment between
Product and Engineering, and less risk of rework once development begins. Those
are the outcomes I care about as a leader: fewer surprises downstream, and a team
that spends its energy building rather than untangling.

## The leadership lesson

It was also a useful reminder that leadership sometimes means stepping outside
your usual lane. Not to own that work permanently — I'm not the designer, and we're
hiring one for good reason — but to remove a blocker and create a better path for
the team when it needs it. Knowing when to step in, and just as importantly when
to step back out, is part of the job.
